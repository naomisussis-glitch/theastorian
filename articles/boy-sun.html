Boy Sun

By Ellie Wilkinson-Sachs · November 2026

When I was a boy my body was elbows and wind. My legs had miles stored up. Every direction was west for me, and I tried them all to see what I liked best. I left beautiful messes behind, the kind that get you into schools, beds, jobs, and heaven, too. My pockets were full of words I collected like loose change, little coins that could make the bells of laughter clang. When I went to my woods, all the trees offered twiggy hands and bent knees to boost me into the sky, where I could reach the sun and pluck it from the blue stem, eat it in big wet bites like a peach and suck on the pit. And nobody ever got mad, that I ate up all the light, because now I had it in my belly and glowed from the inside out.

Ellie Wilkinson-Sachs is a native Vermonter and a first-year at Smith College, where she intends to study English Language & Literature. Her work has been previously published in Zig Zag Literary Magazine.
