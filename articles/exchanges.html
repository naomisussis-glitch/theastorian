Exchanges

By Henry Osworth · November 2026

There is a forty-year-old woman with a bobbed haircut eating a tangerine on a park bench. She’s digging into the skin with her burgundy fingernails with the pressure of someone marking an X on their mosquito bite. She’s collecting the discarded peels in her lap, but one of them slipped between her legs. She has her phone somewhere tucked away, and all she’s focusing on is her Cutie she bought at Sam’s Club. She is, by far, the most interesting thing about this town. Her name is probably Carrie.

–

Evan has a pretty well-established social media presence. He posts frequently about the failure of the modern man, Roman Catholicism, returning to traditional society, AI-assisted macro-tracking apps, and suffering in silence. He has a while to go until he hits his protein goal for the day.

He was laid off two weeks ago, and has to drive past the Panda Express he worked at on the way home from the Crunch Fitness. He parks his car in the garage. As soon as he gets out of the shower, he lies on the couch in the den.

@modernspartacus just hit two hundred thousand followers on Instagram.

–

No one in this room could tell you the capital of Australia.

“Burgeoning star AYSEGUL has already conquered Turkey. Now she’s coming for the United States. The Thracian pop princess sits down with Teen Vogue to talk moving to LA, her first English-language single ‘Come To Me (Hayde)’ and her greatest musical inspirations.”

The song isn’t selling like the man at the label said it would.

–

A 23&Me kit was just mailed to a colonial-era house in the nice part of Connecticut. English, Scottish, Ashkenazi Jewish, German, and trace amounts of Yemeni.

–

Somebody’s cousin bought a food truck. He poured his life savings into the thing. The whole family lauded his entrepreneurial spirit; the man at the bank did too. It was an interesting concept, the Asian fusion joint he parked downtown next to all of the office buildings. Cousins, aunts, uncles, everyone made a point to stand in line on opening day. The gochujang corn dog-kebab was actually alright. No one went back after that. Last anyone heard, he defaulted on the loan.

–

The New Jersey Irish Catholic family at the check-in desk are bringing way too many bags for Ryanair. The mother is complaining about the Fiumicino gate agents. The father grumbles affirmations to her. There’s a woman with them who is too old to be their daughter, but might still be anyway. She’s the only one asking the workers any questions about checking in for the flight to Lourdes. The son, no older than seventeen, is swiping on Tinder. The youngest daughter, far homelier than the rest, had her mother flip one of their suitcases on its side for her to sit down on while she played on her iPad.

The French couple behind them in line are growing more and more upset every time they pronounce Lourdes as Lords. The couple takes turns guessing which one of them was terminally ill, or otherwise in need of a miracle. They both hoped it was the youngest, but were too afraid to say it to the other.

–

There is a born-and-bred fiorentina who is crying while listening to Taylor Swift.

–

Your mother has just developed a taste for Thai food. On your first visit home of the year, she insists on taking you to the new spot on Front Street. She bids the visibly Latina hostess “sawatdee ka” upon entering the restaurant. When the conversation lulls, she comments on how glad she is that more restaurants like this are opening up in your hometown.

“This is what makes America beautiful,” she says.

I just took the elevator up two floors to pay a visit to the vending machine.

–

The chavviest school-leaver in the North, who first left England on a trip to Ibiza last year, passed her driving test after five tries. When she got her licence in the mail, she took a selfie with it and sent the photo to her group chat.

“guess who just got her license bitchesss”

–

Helen has taken on the name Ilinca since moving to Sunnyside. When she started going to the Romanian church, she soon ditched her old life for her new Transylvanian neighbors. She’s taken to the language surprisingly quickly.

Colin, her old college boyfriend, came to visit today. While well-aware of the growing distance between them, he needed a place to stay in the city. Her apartment smelled very strongly of cabbage, and the Orthodox imagery on her walls seemed a little over-the-top. These new details were less important, however, than the other man in Helen’s apartment.

Pasha, no more than twenty years Helen’s senior, stayed with her from time to time. Only when his wife was being difficult, which apparently was often. While Pasha was there, she never left his side.

This wasn’t a surprise; it was hardly the first time Helen had done this. She had been all sorts of people: a practicing witch, a hijabi cheesemonger, a third-party city council candidate who nearly won. This was just the latest instance.

When Colin tried to break the news that Ilinca wasn’t real, Pasha wouldn’t accept it.

“She’s more Romanian than I’ll ever be.”

Pernilla, a schoolgirl from the very top of Sweden, gets made fun of by classmates for sounding too “hinga dinga durgen” when speaking English. Every day, she asks her parents for a cellphone. When they say no, she begs to be allowed more TV.
